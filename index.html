How to Use a Pad

Step-by-step demonstration

Tips: change every 4–6 hours • wash hands • wrap & bin used pads • keep a spare

Quick Instructions

Follow these simple steps

  1. Wash your hands.
  2. Unwrap & unfold the pad.
  3. Peel the backing to reveal the adhesive.
  4. Center it in your underwear, adhesive down.
  5. Press to stick; wrap wings if present.
  6. Change every 4–6 hours (or sooner if needed).

Menstrual Product Comparison

Find what fits your body & lifestyle

Disposable Pads

Absorbent pads that stick to underwear.

  • Pros: Easy for beginners, good for heavy flow
  • Cons: Can feel bulky, creates waste

Tampons

Absorbent cylinders inserted into the vagina.

  • Pros: Great for sports/swimming
  • Cons: Must change within 8 hours

Menstrual Cups

Reusable silicone cups that collect flow.

  • Pros: Eco-friendly, up to 12 hrs wear
  • Cons: Cleaning & learning curve

Period Underwear

Underwear with built-in absorbent layers.

  • Pros: Reusable, comfortable
  • Cons: Higher upfront cost

Hygiene Best Practices

Stay healthy and comfortable

Daily Care

  • Change every 4–6 hours
  • Wash hands before & after
  • Clean with warm water + mild soap (external only)
  • Always wipe front to back

Disposal

  • Wrap used pads in toilet paper/wrapper
  • Never flush pads or tampons
  • Use bins when available

Clothing & Comfort

  • Breathable cotton underwear
  • Carry a spare pad & underwear

Avoid

  • Scented products on the vulva
  • Douching
  • Tampons > 8 hours

Why Hygiene Matters

Benefits & warning signs

Health Benefits

  • Prevents infections
  • Reduces UTIs
  • Lowers risk of TSS

Comfort & Confidence

  • Prevents irritation & rashes
  • Keeps you active and social
Watch for: severe pain, very heavy bleeding, strong/fishy odor, fever, or unusual discharge — see a healthcare provider.

Anatomy & Cycle

Know your body

Reproductive System Diagram

  • Uterus: Lining thickens monthly; sheds during menstruation.
  • Ovaries: Store eggs; make estrogen & progesterone.
  • Fallopian Tubes: Pathway after ovulation.
  • Cervix: Connects uterus to vagina; mucus changes across cycle.
  • Vagina: Canal where menstrual fluid exits.

Cycle & Hormones

  • Days 1–5: Menstruation
  • Days 1–13: Follicular phase
  • Day 14: Ovulation
  • Days 15–28: Luteal phase

Key Hormones

  • Estrogen: Builds uterine lining (first half).
  • Progesterone: Stabilizes lining post-ovulation.
  • FSH: Stimulates egg development.
  • LH: Triggers ovulation.

Ask a Doctor

Answers by Dr. Vani Venkatachalam (Care & Cure Pediatrics)

Emergency Concerning Normal
1. Why are my periods so different from my friends? Normal
Every girl's body is different. Some have shorter periods than others; some bleed less and some bleed more. Go to the doctor if your period is excessively painful, if it lasts longer than seven days or makes you skip school. These are signs of a hormonal imbalance or excessive menstrual bleeding.
2. Is it bad if I skip a month sometimes? Concerning
Period skipping is normal, especially for the first few years. Your body is still adjusting to it all. Still, if you miss for several months or notice weight change or new facial/body hair, talk to a clinician.
3. Why does my mood get really bad before my period? Concerning
Likely PMS: estrogen & progesterone drop before bleeding starts. Try journaling, talking to someone, and light movement. If it’s severe, ask about PMDD—treatments exist.
4. I feel super tired during my period. Is that normal? Concerning
Common—your body is working and losing iron. Eat iron-rich foods and hydrate. If you feel faint/lightheaded often, a doctor may check your iron (hemoglobin/ferritin).
5. I see clumps or clots—is that bad? Emergency
Small clots (dime-size) can be normal. Constant large clots or soaking pads in minutes can signal heavy bleeding—seek medical care.
6. Can I swim or do sports on my period? Normal
Yes. Tampons or cups are great for swimming; pads for other sports. Exercise can even ease cramps.
7. Why is there an odor on my period? Concerning
Mild smell is normal. Strong or fishy odor can mean infection or not changing often—change regularly and see a clinician if it persists.
8. Is it okay to feel embarrassed? Normal
Totally normal feelings—but periods are healthy and common. Talking about it helps reduce stigma.
9. No pads/tampons—what can I use? Concerning
Use soft, clean cotton cloth—fold thick, change often, wash with soap/water, dry fully (sun if possible). Reusable cloth pads are safe when cleaned properly.
10. How do I stay clean if I can’t shower daily? Normal
Wipe the vulva (outside only) front-to-back with a clean wet cloth 1–2×/day. Choose breathable underwear.
11. Can I keep the same cloth/pad all day? Concerning
Change every 6–8 hours if possible. If you can’t, fold a clean layer over the soiled one, then wash ASAP. Long wear can cause rash/infection.
12. I’m shy to ask or can’t afford products. Normal
Schools, community centers, shelters, and food pantries often have free supplies. A teacher, nurse, or trusted adult can help.
13. Why are cramps worse some months? Concerning
Uterine contractions + stress/hormone shifts. Try heat, movement, hydration. If you vomit or miss school regularly, see a doctor.
14. Why am I bloated before my period? Normal
Temporary water retention from hormones. Hydrate and reduce very salty foods.
15. I bled through my clothes at school—help! Normal
It happens. Tie a sweatshirt around your waist, change your pad, use toilet paper briefly, and keep spare underwear if you can.
16. Using toilet paper instead of a pad? Normal
Only as a very short-term fix. It absorbs poorly and falls apart—switch to a pad or clean cloth ASAP.
17. Nowhere private to change? Normal
Plan small privacy windows—restrooms, nurse’s office, or ask a friend to stand guard. A few minutes is enough.
18. Avoiding rashes when I can’t change often? Concerning
A thin layer of petroleum jelly (if available) reduces friction; wear looser clothes for airflow.
19. Can’t wash hands before/after changing? Concerning
Use sanitizer if possible. If not, touch only the clean side of products and wash as soon as you can.
20. No sun to dry reusable cloths—what then? Normal
Hang them in the cleanest, driest, most ventilated place you have. Make sure they’re fully dry before reuse.
